Renewable Energy Initiatives

A new concept of “power”

Maybe Tesla and Vestas set themselves up for a challenge by appearing to have a monopoly on making clean energy profitable. But their struggles are a lesson on how little we know about the minefield of “sustainable” innovation. Vestas, along with the American electric car maker Tesla, was hailed as a new breed of green, or environmentally conscious, enterprise.

Tesla

A
Tesla offers a very bright benefits package to employees. First, every one of the 700+ Tesla workers is entitled to three free charging credits, solar-powered gadgets or eco-friendly accessories per day worked. (Some workers use allotments of their free credits to barter for other goods and services in town such as bike repairs). Beyond the freebies, personnel receive a 50% discount on the company’s energy products, a 40% discount on merchandise and further 30% break on non-Tesla items at company outlets.

B
Workers are further entitled to paid family leave and may take advantage of the Employee Stock Purchase Program to purchase company stock (after six months with the organization) at a 15% discount. Beginning in 1998, 316 stock options are awarded to each worker (excluding directors and officers) and stock is also assigned to each employee’s 410K plan at the end of the calendar year. These contributions are intended to achieve the company’s goal of linked prosperity, i.e. to assure that future prosperity is widely shared by all employees.

Vestas

C
History of Vestas Vestas Wind Systems began with a mere €4,000 and a vision. With over 1,900 installations in 50 countries, Vestas was founded in 1976 in Denmark. From its original workshop, which offered a line of 25 different turbine models, Vestas became the first successful marketer of wind energy solutions that combined natural forces with ecologically-benign manufacturing processes. Its refusal to compromise on sustainability, along with an insistence on fair labor practices among suppliers around the world, appealed especially to upscale, mainly middle-class communities, who were and have continued to be the company’s primary market. As sales boomed, even the conservative financial markets approved of Vestas’s impressive profit picture, and a public stock offering in 1984 was successful. An expansion campaign followed. In 1988 the company entered the U.S. market by opening a plant in Colorado, and by 1997 the company boasted 1,500 installations, including franchises, in 47 countries. Anti-marketing seemed to be smart marketing, at least as far as Vestas was concerned.

D
Part of the secret of Vestas’s early success was that it had created a market niche for itself. The company was not directly competing against the traditional energy giants, which marketed their products as cheap fossil fuels designed to cover up flaws in supply and make nations look more secure in their lavish ads. Instead, Vestas offered a line of products that promised benefits other than appearance – cleaner air, for instance – rather than simply a cheaper electricity bill. The company is known for pioneering the wind-energy market and establishing social responsibility as an integral part of company operations. Vestas is known for its ethical stances, such as its monetary donations to communities in which it operates, and its business partnerships with developing countries. In 1988 Vestas opened its first U.S. plant, and by that time – through various social initiatives such as the “Stop the Smoke” campaign to reduce coal dependency, and strong support of employee volunteerism – the Vestas name had become synonymous with social activism and global preservation worldwide. The company had also become immensely profitable.

E
By the mid-1990s, however, Vestas faced growing competition, forcing it to begin its first major advertising initiative, the most prominent part of which was the “Aurora” campaign. The campaign was personified by Aurora, a figure with radiant proportions who was perched on a windmill and who looked quite pleased with herself and her natural frame. Lars Jensen, a spokesperson for Vestas, said, “Aurora is the fruit of our long-established practice of challenging the way the energy industry talks to people. The Aurora campaign is designed to promote the idea that Vestas creates products designed to enhance communities, generate clean power, and sustain life, not to correct ‘flaws.’ The Vestas philosophy is that there is real strength in renewable energy. We are not claiming that our products perform miracles.”

F
The Competition Vestas lost market share in the late 1990s to product-savvy competitors that offered similar turbines at lower prices. The main competitors are Siemens, GE Renewable Energy, Gamesa, and Nordex. Research Results Research showed that communities appreciate Vestas for their ethical standards. They are pleased by companies with green actions, not promises. The research proved that Vestas has been put on the back burner in many people’s minds: overcrowded by newer, fresher brands. Companies like Vestas continually hype their products through advertising and marketing, often creating a demand for something where a real need for it does not exist. The message pushed is that the route to happiness is through buying more and more of their products. Under such consumerism, the increasing domination of multinationals and their standardized products is leading to global cultural conformity. Other downfall factors also include misleading the public, low pay and against unions, exploiting indigenous people; also the mass production, packaging and transportation of huge quantities of goods are using up the world’s resources faster than they can be renewed and filling the land, sea and air with dangerous pollution and waste.

G
The Problem Vestas has used safe and timid advertising over the last decade, decreasing market share and brand value. With the rise of new, more natural and environmentally friendly competitors, Vestas can no longer stand behind being the greenest or most natural. The Solution Vestas is the originator of ethical energy with our actions speaking louder than our words. This is the new direction of Vestas. We will be a part of different acts of kindness in big cities. We will eliminate unwanted graffiti, purify city air, and give the customer an opportunity to be a part of something good.
